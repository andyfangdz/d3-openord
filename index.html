What you're seeing

  • Drag curve: Parasite drag rises with speed; induced drag falls with speed. Their sum has a minimum at Vg (best glide, max L/D).
  • Power required: D × V (true). At higher altitude, for the same IAS, true airspeed is higher, so more power is required.
  • Power available: For a propeller aircraft, power at the prop is roughly flat with speed and drops with altitude if normally aspirated.
  • Vx: Speed where thrust excess (Ta − D) is max → steepest climb angle.
  • Vy: Speed where power excess (Pa − Pr) is max → highest climb rate.
  • Altitude effects: As altitude increases, power available falls and power required rises (for a given IAS). Vy moves toward Vg and climb performance drops; Vx also trends toward Vg.
Assumptions: standard day ISA, prop aircraft, steady level for drag curve. Educational model, not for flight planning.
